Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset: ONE finished static pixel-art environment study, Twin Peaks fan RPG, GREAT NORTHERN HOTEL — ROOM 315, interior, for a top-down RPG. Not a native implementation. Reference image 1 (Double R diner) and reference image 2 (Sheriff's Station interior) are the project's approved rooms: use them ONLY for camera, native pixel-cluster scale, sprite proportions, wall height, material legibility, contact shadows and commercial finish. Create a completely different room. Do not carry over the diner's burgundy, cream booths, checker floor or signage, nor the station's green institutional walls, desks or filing cabinets. Produce the room only, no inset, no comparison sheet, no UI, no labels outside the scene.

Primary request: a small upscale-rustic hotel room in a late-20th-century American mountain lodge, at 6:20 in the morning. Quiet, orderly, civil, intimate, slightly uncanny in its stillness. A guest has just got up after sleeping in his clothes. One coherent playable room, not a suite.

Style/medium: polished authored pixel art matching the references. Logical canvas 256x192 shown at exact integer 5x (1280x960), landscape 4:3. Crisp chunky square pixel clusters, dark coloured outlines, flat solid colour planes, sparse crisp highlights, stepped hard-edged light pools. No anti-aliasing, no gradients, no blur, no bloom, no painterly noise, no fine grain. Flat elevated orthographic RPG camera looking north like the references: upright north wall about three tiles (48 px) tall, furniture tops visible from above, screen-aligned floor axes, no isometric rotation, no vanishing point.

Composition: room fills almost the whole image inside a narrow dark border. North wall is the focal wall. On the north wall, left of centre: a double bed with a tall dark walnut headboard, top quilt in a muted slate-teal, rumpled on one side only, a folded lodge blanket at the foot with one simple geometric stripe band in ochre and dark brown. Beside the bed, a small walnut bedside table with a brass lamp under a parchment shade that is STILL SWITCHED ON, the single warm amber light pool in the room falling on the table, pillow and the carpet beside the bed, and a black rotary telephone. North wall centre-right: one broad low window with half-open heavy curtains in a dark forest tone; through it, cold blue-gray dawn and the dark stepped silhouettes of pines; the cold window light lays a pale cool rectangle on the carpet below it. Under the window a small walnut writing desk with a compact tape recorder and an open notebook, chair tucked in. East wall: a walnut dresser with an oval mirror above it, the mirror reflecting only the dim room; a folding luggage stand with a closed suitcase; a closed plain walnut bathroom door drawn only as a door surface. West wall: one small framed print of a lake and pines, nothing else. South wall cut away, with a single centred oak hall door, brass "315" number plate and brass handle, and a clear straight strip of carpet from the foot of the bed to the door. One scale-reference player character standing at the foot of the bed, facing the camera, same proportions as the references' player.

Materials and lighting: dark walnut wainscot and door frames with 1-2 px lighter rims, calmer sage-cream painted upper wall with faint vertical knotty-pine board lines, deep slate-teal carpet with one quiet darker border band, brass fittings as single warm highlights, bedding as broad flat planes with a few crease lines, curtains as heavy vertical folds in 3 values, glass as dark cool plane with 1-3 stepped reflections. Exactly two light roles: cool dawn from the window (sets the ambient plane and the floor rectangle) and one warm tungsten pool from the bedside lamp; no other warm source, no ceiling light, no candles. Tight 1-2 px contact shadows under every piece of furniture and the player. Value order: window plane and lamp pool brightest, then bed and quilt, then wood furniture, then walls, carpet quietest. Restrained density: about eight readable objects, generous open floor, no clutter, no stacks, no extra plants, no antlers, no owls, no neon, no signage, no posters.

Preserve the same game's visual identity above all: it must obviously be the same game as the two references (pixel scale, camera, sprite, cluster discipline) while having its own identity: warm walnut, slate-teal, cold dawn, one amber lamp. This is one strong composition for visual approval, not photorealism, not 3D, not a vector illustration.
