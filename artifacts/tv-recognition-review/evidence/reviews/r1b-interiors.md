# Independent TV-interior recognition audit

**Verdict: BAR_WINS.** The references still win on source-specific identity. Only the Double R and Red Room meet the aspirational identity floor of 8/10; the eight-location identity mean is 6.6/10. This is a visual review of the supplied captures only. Text labels and character identity do not count toward recognition. The two Palmer captures are one location with byte-identical images in ACT1_TOWN and ACT4_AFTERNOON; no lighting variant is established. Confidence reflects how directly the supplied frames establish the location’s visual language, not confidence in an unseen floorplan.

Scores are **identity / material-composition / readability**, each out of 10. Readability scores the 256×192 gameplay view. A high readability score does not imply a close set match. Top-down framing is a gameplay constraint; I do not count the camera angle itself as a mismatch.

## Three clearest misses

1. **Room 315:** the visible timber enclosure is weak in the game view. The teal plane and bright window dominate this capture, but no flooring or stable exposure claim follows from the single broadcast angle.
2. **Palmer home:** the supplied pilot views establish a pale upholstered room with oval mirrors, while the game capture reads as a darker wood-and-rug interior.
3. **One Eyed Jacks:** its casino furniture is present, but the saturated pink surfaces lose the broadcast frames’ dark red-brown, draped, low-lit atmosphere.

The sheriff score is also low, but confidence is deliberately low because the only supplied sheriff reference is the reception area and does not establish the office layout.

## Editorial evidence corrections

Following the independent final report review, the lead removed unsupported Palmer night/lighting-variant descriptions and Room 315 flooring/exposure prescriptions. Original scores are unchanged. These corrections narrow claims to the supplied evidence; they are not new art passes.
