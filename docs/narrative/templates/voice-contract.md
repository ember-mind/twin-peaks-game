# Voice contract — inline in the act design §11 or `artifacts/<milestone>/voice-contracts.md`

PURPOSE: make each speaker attributable by mechanism, so the speaker-swap probe passes without names or job words.
WHEN REQUIRED: only for speakers with new or rewritten material. Dialogue-free beats: none.

FIELDS per speaker (one paragraph each, in this order): attention (what they notice first) · protected value · tactic under pressure · disclosure strategy (volunteers / hides / how it leaks) · syntax and rhythm (sentence length, connectives, what ends a turn) · politeness under pressure · the admission they avoid · relationship to this interlocutor (address form, role: partner / guide / adversary / equal) · PATTERN (one sentence with a quoted example that shows the mechanism) · budget heuristics (e.g. one aphorism per scene).

PASS: a cheap critic can anonymise 3+ lines per speaker and the lead attributes them naming the mechanism; two speakers in the same cluster never share attention + tactic + disclosure; mannerism budget stated.
FAIL: the contract is a lexicon ("says 'a verbale' a lot"); an accent or catchphrase is the only differentiator; a "short factual man" that fits two characters.

WHAT NOT TO PUT HERE: backstory, what the character knows (fact ledger), lines to reuse (scene contract §10).
